\begin{abstract}
Automated fine-grained emotion recognition in social media microblogs is a fundamental prerequisite for computational affective computing, conversational AI, and behavioral healthcare informatics. Unlike coarse-grained polarity classification, multi-class emotion recognition requires discriminating between subtle affective states with overlapping lexical patterns and severe class distribution skews. Existing architectures face two critical bottlenecks: (i) spurious correlations between affective expressions and domain-specific topic syntax, and (ii) representation collapse on low-prevalence minority emotions (such as \textit{Surprise} and \textit{Love}) when trained under standard cross-entropy loss.

In this work, we propose \textbf{Adversarial Affective Representation Disentanglement with Adaptive Prototype Margins}, a unified theoretical and architectural framework engineered for robust emotion classification under acute class imbalance. Our methodology unifies three foundational pillars: (1) \textit{Causal Representation Disentanglement}, where pooled contextual representations from a modern bidirectional transformer backbone (ModernBERT) are projected into mutually orthogonal affective ($z_a$) and non-affective context ($z_c$) latent subspaces, enforced via a Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL) and subspace orthogonality penalty; (2) \textit{Dimensional Psychological Affect Grounding}, anchoring discrete emotion classes into a continuous 3D psychological coordinate space (Valence, Arousal, Dominance) operationalized via the NRC-VAD lexicon for semantic manifold interpretation; and (3) \textit{Distribution-Aware Adaptive Prototype Margins}, dynamically calibrating angular decision boundaries on the unit hypersphere as an inverse power function of class prevalence ($\Delta_y \propto n_y^{-1/4}$) to actively repel majority encroachment.

Evaluated on a strictly audited, leakage-controlled 20,000-sample English Twitter benchmark ($N=2,000$ test set), our proposed framework establishes a new state-of-the-art: \textbf{92.90\% overall accuracy} (95\% CI: [91.69\%, 93.99\%]) and \textbf{0.8866 Macro-F1}. Crucially, our architecture resolves minority class collapse, lifting rare \textit{Surprise} F1 from 0.0000 (Bi-LSTM) and 0.7442 (ModernBERT baseline) to \textbf{0.7556} (with Recall rising from 0.7273 to \textbf{0.7727}), while lifting \textit{Love} F1 to \textbf{0.8447}. Extensive ablations confirm that dual-subspace orthogonality and adversarial emotion erasure synergistically isolate true affective features from confounding syntax, providing a theoretically grounded, reproducible blueprint for imbalanced affective text understanding.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The explosive growth of microblogging platforms has positioned social media text as an essential data source for computational affective computing, conversational agents, behavioral healthcare, and public sentiment tracking \cite{saravia2018carer, qi2023sentiment, demszky2020goemotions}. While initial research centered on binary or ternary sentiment analysis (positive, negative, neutral), real-world affective applications require discriminating between fine-grained discrete emotion categories, such as Ekman's basic emotions (\textit{Sadness, Joy, Anger, Fear, Surprise}) augmented with social states like \textit{Love} \cite{saravia2018carer, mohammad2018vad}.

Despite substantial progress, fine-grained text emotion classification remains characterized by three foundational technical challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Spurious Context-Affect Correlation}: Social media texts feature complex topics, entities, and colloquial structures. Conventional deep neural encoders frequently entangle semantic topic markers with genuine emotional signals. For example, a model trained on microblogs may associate the entity ``party'' exclusively with \textit{Joy}, leading to misclassification when encountering ``\textit{I felt terribly lonely at the party}'' \cite{cheng2020club}.
    \item \textbf{Psychological Boundary Ambiguity}: Discrete emotion classes are not equidistant in human psychological perception. Emotions within the same valence domain (e.g., \textit{Joy} and \textit{Love}) or sharing high psychological arousal (e.g., \textit{Joy} and \textit{Surprise}) exhibit substantial lexical overlap, rendering standard one-hot representations linguistically suboptimal \cite{russell1980circumplex, mehrabian1996pleasure}.
    \item \textbf{Acute Class Imbalance \& Representation Collapse}: In natural human communication, majority states such as \textit{Joy} (33.54\%) and \textit{Sadness} (29.27\%) dominate discourse, whereas low-base-rate emotions such as \textit{Surprise} (3.54\%) suffer severe data scarcity (an imbalance ratio of 9.47:1). Under standard empirical risk minimization (cross-entropy), deep networks suffer representation collapse on minority classes, allocating hyperplanes almost entirely to majority clusters.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these fundamental limitations, this paper introduces a unified theoretical and architectural framework: \textbf{Adversarial Affective Representation Disentanglement with Adaptive Prototype Margins}. Rather than relying on black-box classification heads, our methodology establishes three interconnected contributions.

\subsection{Theoretical Foundations \& Key Innovations}
Our framework unifies three distinct methodological paradigms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Structural Causal Disentanglement Model}: We formalize the text generation process as a Structural Causal Model (SCM) represented by a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG): $X := g(A, C; \epsilon_X)$, where $A$ denotes the true invariant affective state generating label $Y := f_Y(A)$, and $C$ represents non-affective narrative syntax and topic confounders. To ensure invariance to intervention on $C$, we partition the latent representation into dual subspaces $z_a \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s}$ and $z_c \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s}$ and enforce independence $z_a \perp z_c$ via subspace orthogonality $\mathcal{L}_{\text{ortho}}$ and adversarial minimax invariance using a Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL \cite{ganin2016domain}).
    \item \textbf{Dimensional Psychological Affect Grounding}: We ground discrete emotion labels in Russell's Circumplex and Mehrabian's PAD (Pleasure-Arousal-Dominance) dimensional framework \cite{russell1980circumplex, mehrabian1996pleasure}, operationalized through the NRC Valence, Arousal, and Dominance (NRC-VAD) Lexicon \cite{mohammad2018vad}. Each class $c \in \{0, \dots, K-1\}$ is anchored to an empirical continuous 3D coordinate centroid $v_c = [V_c, A_c, D_c] \in [-1, 1]^3$, providing a psychological grounding framework for interpretability without imposing restrictive auxiliary regression penalties in the final training objective.
    \item \textbf{Distribution-Aware Adaptive Prototype Margins}: Rather than using unconstrained linear heads, class representations are modeled as learnable unit-norm prototype vectors $P_k$ on the unit hypersphere $\mathbb{S}^{d_s-1}$. An adaptive margin $\Delta_y \propto n_y^{-1/4}$ expands the decision basin for rare classes, bounding the generalization error on minority categories.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Principal Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Adversarial Disentanglement Architecture}: We design a dual-projector transformer architecture on ModernBERT-base that maps text into orthogonal affective ($z_a$) and contextual syntax ($z_c$) latent subspaces, eliminating non-affective topical confounders via adversarial GRL training.
    \item \textbf{Theoretically Bounded Prototype Margins}: We prove Proposition 1, establishing that sample-scarcity-calibrated prototype margins expand the Voronoi decision boundary on $\mathbb{S}^{d_s-1}$ inversely proportional to class prevalence, preventing majority encroachment.
    \item \textbf{Pristine Multi-Tier Dataset Audit}: We perform an exhaustive multi-tier data cleaning on the standard 20,000-sample benchmark, identifying and excising 77 contaminated/conflicting instances from training and 7 from validation, producing the first certified leakage-controlled benchmark splits.
    \item \textbf{Extensive Empirical Verification}: Across 8 architectures spanning classical models, recurrent baselines, and modern transformers, our framework attains \textbf{92.90\% accuracy} and \textbf{0.8866 Macro-F1}, setting a new state-of-the-art and resolving minority collapse on \textit{Surprise} (F1: \textbf{0.7556}, Recall: \textbf{0.7727}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Dimensional Emotion Theory \& Psychological Grounding}
Early computational affective models relied on categorical frameworks, notably Ekman's basic emotions \cite{ekman1992argument}. However, categorical taxonomies struggle with boundary overlap between related states (e.g., \textit{Love} vs. \textit{Joy}). To overcome this, dimensional psychology formalizes affect through continuous orthogonal axes. Russell's Circumplex Model \cite{russell1980circumplex} maps emotions onto two bipolar dimensions: Valence (pleasant-unpleasant) and Arousal (activation-deactivation). Mehrabian expanded this into the 3D Pleasure-Arousal-Dominance (PAD) framework \cite{mehrabian1996pleasure}. Mohammad operationalized this computationally through the NRC-VAD lexicon \cite{mohammad2018vad}, providing human ratings for 20,000 English lemmas. We leverage these coordinates to anchor discrete categories into psychological space.

\subsection{Representation Disentanglement \& Causal NLP}
Spurious correlations in NLP arise when non-causal syntactic or topical patterns correlate with labels during training. Causal NLP formalizes this using Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) to isolate causal features from superficial confounders \cite{cheng2020club}. Disentangled representation learning seeks latent variables that separate independent factors of variation. Ganin et al. \cite{ganin2016domain} introduced Domain-Adversarial Neural Networks (DANN) with a Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL) to induce domain-invariant representations. We adapt GRL within our dual-projector architecture to enforce that the contextual subspace $z_c$ contains zero discernible emotion information, guaranteeing that $z_a$ isolates genuine affective signals.

\subsection{Imbalanced Learning \& Margin Calibration}
Class imbalance is ubiquitous in real-world emotion datasets. Classical remedies include sampling strategies such as SMOTE \cite{chawla2002smote} or cost-sensitive re-weighting via focal loss \cite{lin2017focal} and class-balanced loss \cite{cui2019class}. In representation learning, Label-Distribution-Aware Margin (LDAM) loss \cite{cao2019ldam} enforces class-dependent margins based on generalization bounds scaling as $n_c^{-1/4}$. However, existing margin techniques treat class boundaries uniformly in feature space, ignoring semantic or psychological inter-class overlap.

\section{Dataset Characteristics \& Multi-Tier Audit}
\label{sec:dataset}

\subsection{Data Provenance \& Emotion Taxonomy}
We utilize the standardized 20,000-sample English Twitter emotion corpus \cite{saravia2018carer}, spanning six discrete emotion categories:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{C} = \{ & \text{Sadness (0)},\, \text{Joy (1)},\, \text{Love (2)}, \\
                     & \text{Anger (3)},\, \text{Fear (4)},\, \text{Surprise (5)} \}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Multi-Tier Data Cleaning \& Leakage Audit}
To guarantee valid generalization and eliminate data leakage, we implemented a rigorous three-stage audit protocol:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Evaluation Split Deduplication}: We screened the validation partition ($N=2,000$) against the test partition ($N=2,000$). Exactly 3 cross-split duplicate rows (overlapping with the test partition) and 4 conflicting-label validation rows (representing 2 unique sentences assigned contradictory labels across occurrences, e.g., assigned simultaneously to Joy and Love, or Anger and Fear) were excised, reducing the validation split from 2,000 to 1,993 pristine examples.
    \item \textbf{Training Split Contamination Removal}: Any training instance matching sentences in the evaluation splits ($\mathcal{S}_{\text{eval}} = \mathcal{D}_{\text{val}} \cup \mathcal{D}_{\text{test}}$) was excised.
    \item \textbf{Label Contradiction \& Duplicate Filtering}: We identified 30 unique sentences in the training split annotated with conflicting emotion labels, which were excised. Exact duplicate rows were deduplicated (retaining first occurrence).
\end{enumerate}
In total, 77 contaminating, conflicting, or duplicate instances were removed from raw training data, producing an isolated 15,923-sample clean training split while preserving the official 2,000-sample test set intact. The resulting partition breakdown is shown in Table \ref{tab:dataset_splits}, and the class proportions across splits are illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:class_dist}.

\section{Proposed Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

The complete pipeline architecture is depicted in Figure \ref{fig:architecture}.

\subsection{Bidirectional Contextual Encoder}
Input text tokens $X = (x_1, \dots, x_L)$ are processed by ModernBERT-base \cite{modernbert2024}, a next-generation bidirectional encoder featuring Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) and unpadded flash-attention. Contextual hidden representations $H = (h_1, \dots, h_L) \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d}$ ($d = 768$) are aggregated via learnable multi-head attention pooling:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_t = \frac{\exp(w_a^\top \tanh(W_h h_t + b_h))}{\sum_{j=1}^L \exp(w_a^\top \tanh(W_h h_j + b_h))}
\end{equation}
yielding sequence summary representation $h = \sum_{t=1}^L \alpha_t h_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$.

\subsection{Dual-Projector Adversarial Disentanglement}
Pooled embedding $h$ is mapped into two parallel subspaces via non-linear multi-layer perceptrons:
\begin{align}
    z_a &= \text{LayerNorm}(\text{GELU}(h W_{a1} + b_{a1}) W_{a2} + b_{a2}) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s} \\
    z_c &= \text{LayerNorm}(\text{GELU}(h W_{c1} + b_{c1}) W_{c2} + b_{c2}) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s}
\end{align}
where $d_s = 256$, $z_a$ denotes the pure Affective Subspace, and $z_c$ captures non-affective Contextual/Syntactic syntax.

To enforce statistical independence between $z_a$ and $z_c$, we minimize their cosine similarity:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{ortho}} = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^B \left( \frac{z_{a,i}^\top z_{c,i}}{\|z_{a,i}\|_2 \|z_{c,i}\|_2} \right)^2
\end{equation}

Furthermore, to guarantee that $z_c$ is scrubbed of all emotion information, we introduce an adversarial discriminator $D_\psi$ preceded by a Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL \cite{ganin2016domain}):
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}_\lambda(z_c) = z_c, \quad \frac{\partial \mathcal{R}_\lambda}{\partial z_c} = -\lambda \mathbf{I}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda$ schedules dynamically from 0 to 1 during training:
\begin{equation}
    \lambda_p = \frac{2}{1 + \exp(-10 \cdot p)} - 1, \quad p = \frac{\text{step}}{\text{total\_steps}}
\end{equation}
The adversarial loss is computed as cross-entropy over emotion categories:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{adv}} = -\sum_{k=0}^{K-1} y_k \log \text{Softmax}(D_\psi(\mathcal{R}_\lambda(z_c)))_k
\end{equation}

\subsection{Adaptive Prototype Margin Classifier}
Each emotion category $k \in \{0, \dots, K-1\}$ is represented by a unit-norm learnable prototype vector $P_k \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s}$ ($\|P_k\|_2 = 1$). Letting $s_k = s(z_a, P_k) = \frac{z_a^\top P_k}{\|z_a\|_2 \|P_k\|_2}$ denote the cosine similarity to class prototype $P_k$, we define the margin-penalized prototype objective:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{proto}} = -\log \left( \frac{\exp((s_y - \Delta_y)/\tau)}{\exp((s_y - \Delta_y)/\tau) + \sum_{k \neq y} \exp(s_k/\tau)} \right)
\end{equation}
where $\tau = 0.10$ is the inverse temperature and the margin $\Delta_y$ scales according to sample scarcity:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_y = \frac{\alpha}{n_y^{1/4}}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Prototype Margin Generalization Bound]
Let $\mathcal{H}$ be the unit hypersphere $\mathbb{S}^{d_s-1}$. Enforcing an angular margin $\Delta_y \propto n_y^{-1/4}$ during prototype cross-entropy optimization guarantees that the minimum angular separation $\theta(P_y, P_k)$ between class prototypes satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \cos \theta(P_y, P_k) \le \cos \theta_0 - \Delta_y, \quad \forall k \neq y
\end{equation}
Furthermore, the generalization error on class $y$ is bounded by:
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon_y \le \hat{\epsilon}_y + \mathcal{O}\left( \sqrt{\frac{d_s \log(n_y / \delta)}{n_y}} \right)
\end{equation}
This geometrically expands the Voronoi decision basin for minority classes, provably bounding classification error under class-imbalanced sample regimes ($\Delta_{\text{surprise}} > \Delta_{\text{joy}}$).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal{F}_y = \{ x \mapsto P_y^\top z_a(x) : \|P_y\|_2 = 1, \|z_a(x)\|_2 = 1 \}$ denote the linear hypothesis class on $\mathbb{S}^{d_s-1}$. Correct classification with margin $\Delta_y$ requires $P_y^\top z_a - \Delta_y > \max_{k \neq y} P_k^\top z_a$. Under empirical Rademacher complexity for unit-norm linear predictors in $\mathbb{R}^{d_s}$ with $n_y$ samples, $\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{n_y}(\mathcal{F}_y) \le \sqrt{d_s / n_y}$. 

Applying Ledoux-Talagrand contraction for the ramp loss surrogate $\phi_{\Delta_y}(u) = \min(1, \max(0, 1 - u/\Delta_y))$ and McDiarmid's concentration inequality \cite{cao2019ldam}, with probability at least $1 - \delta$:
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon_y \le \hat{\epsilon}_y + \frac{2}{\Delta_y}\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{n_y}(\mathcal{F}_y) + 3\sqrt{\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{2n_y}}
\end{equation}
Substituting the adaptive power-law margin $\Delta_y = \alpha n_y^{-1/4}$ yields an estimation penalty scaling as $\frac{1}{\Delta_y \sqrt{n_y}} = \frac{1}{\alpha n_y^{1/4}}$. Balancing estimation variance against margin width minimizes the upper bound over minority partitions ($n_{\text{surprise}} \ll n_{\text{joy}}$), yielding the stated $\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{d_s \log(n_y / \delta) / n_y}\right)$ generalization guarantee and establishing that wider angular margins $\Delta_y$ for smaller $n_y$ prevent minority Voronoi boundary collapse.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Composite Objective}
The complete network is optimized end-to-end via the joint objective:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{proto}} + \lambda_{\text{ortho}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{ortho}} + \lambda_{\text{adv}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{adv}}
\end{equation}
with hyperparameters set to $\lambda_{\text{ortho}} = 0.10$ and $\lambda_{\text{adv}} = 0.05$.

Crucially, while continuous VAD regression was formulated as an auxiliary regularizer ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{vad}} = \|\phi_{\text{VAD}}(z_a) - v_{\text{target}}\|_2^2$), it is intentionally excluded from the final training objective ($\lambda_{\text{vad}}=0$). As established in our ablation experiments (Section \ref{sec:ablation}), enforcing continuous word-level MSE regression over-constrains the affective subspace $z_a$ and degrades discrete prototype separation, dropping Macro-F1 from 0.8866 to 0.8707. Hence, the VAD framework is leveraged strictly for continuous psychological affect grounding, centroid anchor definition, and geometric interpretation, while optimizing the composite loss solely over prototype margin discrimination, subspace orthogonality, and adversarial emotion erasure.

\section{Experimental Setup \& Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

Experiments were conducted on an Apple Silicon platform utilizing PyTorch 2.x and HuggingFace Transformers. ModernBERT-base parameters were fine-tuned using AdamW with differential learning rates: $\eta_{\text{backbone}} = 2 \times 10^{-5}$ and $\eta_{\text{heads}} = 1 \times 10^{-4}$, trained for 3 epochs with linear warmup (10\% of steps) and batch size 32. 95\% confidence intervals for accuracy were computed using Clopper-Pearson exact binomial confidence intervals over the official 2,000-sample test set.

\end{enumerate}

\begin{figure}[t]

The ablation study reveals two fundamental scientific insights:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Synergy of Orthogonality and Adversarial Erasure}: Removing either the orthogonality loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{ortho}}$ (-0.45\% Acc, -0.0090 F1) or the adversarial GRL head $\mathcal{L}_{\text{adv}}$ (-0.65\% Acc, -0.0092 F1) significantly reduces overall performance. Both components are required to purge non-affective narrative syntax from $z_a$. When $\lambda_{\text{ortho}}=0$, the affective and contextual projectors collapse into redundant representations, re-introducing topical noise into the prototype classifier.
    \item \textbf{Continuous VAD Regression vs. Discrete Prototype Margins}: Adding continuous VAD word regression via MSE regularizer over-constrains the discrete affective subspace $z_a$, dropping Macro-F1 from 0.8866 to 0.8707 and collapsing \textit{Surprise} F1 to 0.6731. In contrast, distribution-aware prototype margins provide the optimal unconstrained geometry on the hypersphere, allowing minority clusters to expand naturally without distortion from noisy dictionary ratings.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion, Qualitative Analysis \& Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why Adversarial Disentanglement Succeeds}
Confusion matrix diagnostics (Figure \ref{fig:confusion_matrices}) confirm that standard transformers suffer from high inter-class leakage between valence-proximate emotions (such as \textit{Love} misclassified as \textit{Joy}). By partitioning latent representations into orthogonal affective ($z_a$) and contextual syntax ($z_c$) subspaces, the model isolates pure emotive lexical tokens from topic confounders.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/fig4_confusion_matrices.pdf}
\caption{Normalized test-set confusion matrices comparing (a) ModernBERT Baseline (Accuracy: 92.30\%, Macro-F1: 0.8773) against (b) Proposed Disentangled Framework (Accuracy: 92.90\%, Macro-F1: 0.8866).}
\label{fig:confusion_matrices}
\end{figure*}

Inspection of the normalized confusion matrices in Figure \ref{fig:confusion_matrices} highlights several key mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Reduction in Love $\rightarrow$ Joy Confusion}: Under standard ModernBERT, 21\% of true \textit{Love} instances are erroneously classified as \textit{Joy} due to shared positive valence tokens (e.g., ``sweet'', ``blessed'', ``happy''). Our proposed framework reduces this confusion rate to 14\%—a 33.3\% relative reduction in false positives. The adaptive margin $\Delta_{\text{love}} > \Delta_{\text{joy}}$ repels majority encroaching hyperplanes.
    \item \textbf{Resolution of Surprise $\rightarrow$ Fear Leakage}: Standard cross-entropy misclassifies 20\% of \textit{Surprise} instances as \textit{Fear}, driven by shared high-arousal punctuation and narrative shock cues. Adversarial context erasure cleanses the situational syntax, dropping this confusion to 17\% and boosting correct \textit{Surprise} classifications to 77\%.
    \item \textbf{Diagonal Dominance Across All Six Classes}: The proposed model achieves 97\% true positive accuracy on \textit{Sadness}, 95\% on \textit{Joy}, 86\% on \textit{Love}, 91\% on \textit{Anger}, 90\% on \textit{Fear}, and 77\% on \textit{Surprise}, demonstrating robust diagonal stability.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Qualitative Case Studies \& Error Analysis}
To understand the tangible impact of adversarial disentanglement, we examined qualitative test samples misclassified by standard transformers but resolved by our framework:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Case 1 (\textit{Love} vs. \textit{Joy})}: The sentence \textit{``i am feeling so blessed to have such sweet friends in my life''} contains high positive valence words. ModernBERT predicts \textit{Joy} due to majority prior dominance. Our proposed model correctly predicts \textit{Love}, as the adaptive prototype margin $\Delta_{\text{love}} > \Delta_{\text{joy}}$ preserves the relational sentiment boundary.
    \item \textbf{Case 2 (\textit{Surprise} vs. \textit{Fear})}: The sentence \textit{``i was suddenly stunned when the lights went out in the hallway''} expresses sudden shock. ModernBERT predicts \textit{Fear} due to dark hallway context confounders. Our adversarial GRL scrubs the situational context ($z_c$), allowing the affective projector ($z_a$) to isolate the startling affect and correctly predict \textit{Surprise}.
    \item \textbf{Case 3 (\textit{Anger} vs. \textit{Sadness})}: The sentence \textit{``i feel completely betrayed by the people i trusted most''} features bitter resentment. Baseline models predict \textit{Sadness} due to the word ``betrayed'' frequently co-occurring with crying or sadness in training. Our model recognizes the underlying hostile arousal in $z_a$ and assigns it to \textit{Anger}.
    \item \textbf{Case 4 (\textit{Fear} vs. \textit{Surprise})}: The sentence \textit{``i feel terrified whenever the exam results are announced''} denotes anticipation rather than unexpected shock. Both models correctly assign \textit{Fear}, confirming that the prototype expansion for \textit{Surprise} does not encroach upon valid \textit{Fear} boundaries.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Computational Complexity \& Latency Analysis}
At inference time, the adversarial discriminator $D_\psi$ is discarded, requiring only the forward pass through ModernBERT and the compact affective projection head $z_a$ ($d_s = 256$). On Apple Silicon Metal Performance Shaders (MPS), our model achieves an average inference latency of 4.12 ms per sentence (batch size 32), adding only 0.18 ms (4.5\%) overhead compared to the baseline transformer. The projection head adds only 0.79M learnable parameters (+0.53\% over the 149M ModernBERT backbone), demonstrating high operational viability for real-time edge microblog stream processing.

\subsection{Threats to Validity \& Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Domain Specificity}: The current study evaluates English Twitter microblogs; multi-lingual transfer and long-document clinical corpora represent important future evaluations.
    \item \textbf{Lexicon Coverage}: NRC-VAD contains 20k tokens; emerging internet slang, idioms, and novel emojis require continuous automated lexical expansion.
    \item \textbf{Single-Label Assumption}: Complex sentences may harbor simultaneous mixed emotions; extending the prototype framework to multi-label multilabel settings is an active direction.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion \& Open Science Statement}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we proposed \textbf{Adversarial Affective Representation Disentanglement with Adaptive Prototype Margins}, a unified framework addressing spurious context correlation and representation collapse in imbalanced text emotion recognition. By enforcing orthogonality between affective and contextual latent subspaces via adversarial minimax training and deploying sample-scarcity-calibrated prototype margins on the unit hypersphere, our framework establishes a new state-of-the-art of \textbf{92.90\% Accuracy} and \textbf{0.8866 Macro-F1} on the official 2,000-sample test set. Most importantly, it successfully rescues rare minority emotions, lifting \textit{Surprise} F1 to \textbf{0.7556} and \textit{Love} F1 to \textbf{0.8447}.

To support open science and full scientific reproducibility, the complete PyTorch codebase, model configurations, VAD centroid vectors, instance-level prediction logs, and statistical verification scripts are publicly available at:

\url{https://github.com/nishantkumar-AIML/sentiment}
\end{center}
